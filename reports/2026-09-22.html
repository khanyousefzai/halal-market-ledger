How to read the ratios

Colour bands apply to every table and card below

Five ratios drive everything here. Each is colour-coded the same way wherever it appears: jade is healthy, amber is acceptable but worth watching, red is a flag that needs a reason.

P/E

Price to Earnings

What you pay for each dollar the company earns. A P/E of 20 means twenty years of current earnings to pay back the share price. Low can mean cheap — or that earnings are expected to fall.

below 20cheap
20 – 50premium
above 50expensive

P/B

Price to Book

Share price against the accounting value of net assets. Useful for banks and miners; misleading for asset-light firms and any company that has bought back a lot of stock, which shrinks book equity toward zero.

below 3asset-backed
3 – 15typical
above 15thin

ROE

Return on Equity

Profit generated per dollar of shareholder capital. The best quick measure of business quality — but check debt alongside it, because leverage inflates ROE without improving the business.

above 30%excellent
10 – 30%solid
below 10%weak

D/E

Debt to Equity

Borrowings against shareholder capital. Central to Shariah screening, though the AAOIFI test measures debt against market capitalisation with a 33% ceiling — not against book equity as shown here.

below 0.5low
0.5 – 1.5moderate
above 1.5leveraged

Curr

Current Ratio

Short-term assets against short-term bills. Below 1.0 means the company owes more within a year than it holds in liquid assets — survivable for a cash-generative giant, dangerous for anyone else.

above 2.0strong
1.0 – 2.0adequate
below 1.0tight

The 105-point score

Every one of the 100 stocks is scored identically. Six components are graded on sliding scales — no arbitrary cut-offs inside a component — and summed. The weighting deliberately puts more on the balance sheet (D/E plus current ratio = 35 points) than on the analyst target (26 points), because forecasts are opinions and the balance sheet is a fact.

Shariah compliance notes

The screens genuinely disagree. SPUS follows the S&P 500 Sharia methodology and HLAL the FTSE USA Shariah index. FTSE measures debt against total assets while S&P measures against market cap, so a name can clear one and fail the other — the Screen column in section B records which.

Debt is screened against market capitalisation under AAOIFI, with a 33% ceiling — not against book equity. A high book D/E in section C is therefore not automatically disqualifying. Zoya is looser than either index screen.

Purification. Even compliant companies earn incidental interest income. The standard practice is to calculate the non-compliant portion of dividends received and donate it. Fund providers publish annual purification ratios; for direct holdings you calculate it yourself.

Excluded as non-compliant: MCD, CMG, DE, HON, GE, LMT, RTX, TMUS, DIS, AMGN, MFI. Questionable — screens disagree, decide for yourself: COST, WMT, NFLX, SBUX, INTU, CAT, ATD, L, MRU, WN, DOL, PBH.
